Chapter 3

Estimates and contracts: what the paper must say

The legal minimum, the pricing types and a five-point read of any estimate you are handed.

An estimate is the number you will hold the mover to, and a contract is what lets you do it. Colorado requires both for permitted household goods movers who work inside the state.

What Colorado puts on the page

The PUC's consumer page lists what a legal intrastate moving contract carries:

  • The mover's permit number, which starts with HHG, on every contract and in every advertisement.
  • A statement that the mover is permitted with the Colorado Public Utilities Commission, with the number.
  • An itemized breakdown and description of all costs, rates and services, including accessorial services, for transportation and storage of household goods.
  • The forms of payment the mover accepts. The PUC says the law requires acceptance of at least two of: cash, cashier's check, valid personal check or credit card.
  • A written consumer advisement, handed over at or before the time work begins, covering your rights and the arbitration process.

The statute behind this is C.R.S. 40-10.1-505. A PUC consumer poster adds that the written estimate must show total costs and the cost basis, and must reach you at least 24 hours before the move unless you requested the move with less than 24 hours of notice.

Three ways a mover can price you

TypeWhat you are quotedWhat to watch
HourlyA rate for a crew and truck, plus any minimum hours, travel time and feesAsk how the clock starts and stops, and whether travel time between your two addresses is billed
BindingA fixed price agreed in writing for a defined list of items and services (federal term)The inventory it covers; extras you add on the day are usually priced on top
Non-bindingA best guess based on weight or hoursUnder federal rules for interstate moves, you cannot be required to pay more than 110 percent of the estimate at delivery; the rest is billed later

The 110 percent protection is a federal interstate rule described by FMCSA. We did not verify an identical cap in the Colorado intrastate rules, so for a local hourly or non-binding job, ask the mover to write down how it handles a final bill above the estimate.

Read the estimate like an inspector

  1. Does it match what you showed them? A written estimate without a survey is a weak document. The PUC recommends an in-person inspection for accuracy.
  2. Are services itemized? Packing, stairs, long carries, shuttle trucks, storage and fuel or travel charges should each have a line, not a lump.
  3. Is the permit number there? Look it up again against the PUC search. A number copied onto the paper is not proof.
  4. What is the deposit? Be wary of large cash deposits demanded before any paperwork exists.
  5. What protection is included? Ask what the mover's liability for loss or damage is and what an upgrade costs. See Chapter 5.

Rate quote is not an estimate. FMCSA points out that a mover's "rate quote" does not count as an estimate. If someone gives you only a per-pound or per-hour figure, ask for a full written estimate.
