Chapter 4

Red flags, from the first call to moving day

The patterns regulators warn about, so you can recognise them while you can still walk away.

Most moving trouble announces itself early. These are the signs, grouped by when you spot them. One alone is a reason to ask questions; two or three together is a reason to hang up.

On the first call

  • No permit or registration number offered. In Colorado the HHG number belongs on the company's ads. An advertisement without it is out of line with what the PUC describes.
  • A price with no questions asked. If nobody asks how many rooms, what floor, whether there is an elevator or what is in the garage, the number is made up.
  • Pressure to book today. "Prices go up tomorrow" is a sales line, not a rate card.
  • A generic greeting. Answering with only "moving company" or "movers" rather than a business name can mean you reached a lead broker, not the company that sends the truck. Ask whose trucks and crews will do the job.

In the paperwork

  • No written estimate, or one with a single lump-sum line. Colorado requires a written estimate with the total costs and their basis.
  • Blank spaces on a contract you are asked to sign. Do not sign anything incomplete.
  • Cash only, or a large deposit up front. Colorado requires permitted movers to accept at least two of cash, cashier's check, valid personal check or credit card. A credit card also gives you a dispute path that cash does not.
  • Coverage language you were not walked through. Released-value coverage under the federal system pays by weight, at a rate FMCSA describes as 60 cents per pound per article, which is rarely close to replacement value.

On moving day

  • An unmarked or rented truck, or a different company name than on your contract. Ask who is employing the crew in front of you.
  • A new, higher price at the door. Stop and ask for the change in writing, before anything else leaves your home.
  • Refusal to unload until you pay more than agreed. The PUC accepts complaints about refusal to return property. Write down times and names, photograph the truck and plates, and see Chapter 5.

What to do if you spot one

Before anything is loaded, you can cancel and call someone else; a lost deposit is cheaper than a lost sofa. After loading, document everything, stay calm and contact the PUC (303-894-2070) for a local move or the federal channels for an interstate one. Do not confront a crew in a way that risks your safety; call local law enforcement if you believe your goods are being held or stolen.
