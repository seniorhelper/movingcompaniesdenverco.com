Chapter 6

A Denver year of moving

Which months bring snow, hail or peak demand, and what to line up before the truck arrives.

The date you pick shapes availability, how much leverage you have, and which Denver logistics you must solve in advance. Below is a practical calendar. Where a point comes from a source it is cited; where it is general experience of the moving trade, we say so.

Season by season

SeasonWhat to expect
Winter (Dec to Feb)Snow can delay a crew, and an icy driveway or alley slows the carry. Book a backup day. Trips into the mountains may fall under winter traction rules (below).
Spring (Mar to May)Demand builds toward summer. Hail and storms are a Front Range feature of this season, so protect furniture during loading and ask how the truck is kept dry. (General observation, not a sourced forecast.)
Summer (Jun to Aug)The industry's busiest stretch. FreightWaves Checkpoint refers to peak moving season and says a last-minute booking in that season can raise rates. Heat and altitude make a long carry tiring; schedule early starts.
Fall (Sep to Nov)Demand generally eases after Labor Day. The commercial-vehicle chain law period on I-70 begins September 1.

Month-end and weekends

Leases commonly turn over at the end of the month, and weekends are the days most people are off work. We have no Denver-specific count, so treat this as the general pattern in the trade: the first and last days of a month and any Saturday are the hardest to book and the least flexible on price. A weekday in the middle of a month gives you the widest choice of dates.

Your Denver checklist by lead time

Six to eight weeks out

  • Run the ten-minute check on every company you plan to invite for an estimate.
  • Ask your building's management what it needs: certificate of insurance, reserved elevator time, loading dock rules and any restricted hours.

Three to four weeks out

  • Get written estimates. Book the winning company and confirm the date in the contract.
  • If a truck must occupy a Denver street or a metered space, ask the city's Department of Transportation and Infrastructure how, and how far ahead, to apply for a street-occupancy or meter request. We have not verified the current lead time or fee, so confirm both with the city.

The last week

  • Confirm the arrival window, the crew size and the name on the truck.
  • Clear the parking spot, reserve the elevator, and photograph high-value items.

Mountain trips and winter rules

If your job sends a commercial truck up I-70, note that Colorado's chain law for commercial vehicles is reported to run on I-70 between Dotsero and Morrison from September 1 through May 31. Ask any mover heading west about chains and how they plan for closures. Check the CDOT chain law page for the current wording before you rely on those dates.
