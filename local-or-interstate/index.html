Chapter 2

Which rulebook applies to your move

State line or no state line: the answer picks your regulator, your checks and your complaint route.

Two different agencies watch movers, and which one applies decides where you verify the company, which paperwork you are owed and where you complain. The dividing line is simple: where the shipment starts and ends.

Your moveRegulatorWhere to verifyWhere to complain
Starts and ends in Colorado (Denver to Colorado Springs, Aurora to Fort Collins)Colorado Public Utilities Commission, under Title 40, Article 10.1, C.R.S., and PUC rulesPUC permit search; permit number starts with HHGPUC complaint form or 303-894-2070
Crosses a state line (Denver to Salt Lake City, Denver to Cheyenne)U.S. DOT, via the Federal Motor Carrier Safety AdministrationFMCSA Protect Your Move, search by USDOT numberFMCSA's national consumer complaint channels

What this means in practice

The PUC only covers moves inside Colorado

The PUC says plainly that it regulates intrastate moves; for interstate ones it points consumers to the U.S. Department of Transportation. That is why a Denver mover can be properly permitted with the state and still be the wrong choice for your cross-country job if it holds no federal authority.

Paperwork differs

Local: written estimate plus written contract, both required by the PUC. Interstate: the federal rules call for specific documents and estimate types, and FMCSA publishes a consumer booklet explaining your rights before, during and after the move. The PUC itself recommends the FMCSA guide to Colorado consumers.

Disputes differ

For a local move, the PUC's consumer page says refunds for household goods movers must go through binding arbitration under section 40-10.1-507, C.R.S. For interstate moves, the complaint path and claim rules are federal. Chapter 5 goes through both.

Edge cases to ask about

  • Denver to a Wyoming or Kansas town 60 miles away. Still interstate. The distance does not matter, the state line does.
  • A mover that "only does local" but offers to haul your load to Phoenix. Ask to see the federal registration before you agree.
  • Storage in between. If you pick up in Colorado and ship out of state, treat the whole job as interstate and verify federally.
  • Labor-only helpers. A crew that loads your rental truck but does not transport goods is a different arrangement from a licensed mover; ask who is responsible if something is damaged.
