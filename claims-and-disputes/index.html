Chapter 5

Claims and disputes

What to write down at delivery and where a complaint goes if the problem is real.

Most moves finish without a problem. If yours does not, what you do in the first hour matters more than anything you do the following month.

At delivery

  1. Walk through with the inventory in hand before the crew leaves.
  2. Note every missing or damaged item on the paperwork before you sign. A clean signature can be read as a clean delivery.
  3. Photograph damage with the box or furniture in frame and the room around it.
  4. Keep packing materials for damaged items until the claim is settled.

Local moves (inside Colorado)

The PUC takes complaints about fraud, overcharging, damaged goods and refusal to return property by an intrastate mover. Its consumer page also notes that, per statute (section 40-10.1-507, C.R.S.), refunds for household goods movers must go through binding arbitration. The mover is required to hand you a written consumer advisement describing your rights and the arbitration process at or before the time work begins; keep your copy. The call-in line is 303-894-2070.

We have not confirmed the exact claim-filing deadline that applies to a local move, so read the contract and the consumer advisement for it and write the date on your calendar when you receive the paperwork.

Interstate moves

  • Nine months. FMCSA tells consumers they have nine months from the date of delivery to file a written claim with the mover.
  • Coverage choice. The default, released value, is described by FMCSA as the most economical option, at no extra charge, but it limits payment by weight rather than by what an item cost. Full value protection is the alternative, priced separately. Decide before the move, not after.
  • Written, itemized claim. Describe each item, its value and the damage, and attach photographs and the inventory page.

Insurance you can buy elsewhere

If you want protection beyond what the mover's liability offers, ask your homeowner's or renter's insurer what travels with your goods and whether a separate transit policy is worth its cost. That is a call to your insurer, not a decision we can make for you.

Keep a file. Estimate, contract, inventory, delivery receipt, photos, emails and texts. When a dispute reaches the PUC or a claims adjuster, whoever has the cleaner paper trail usually has the easier time.
