A field guide for people about to hire movers in Denver

How to choose a Denver moving company without getting burned

Six short chapters, written around what Colorado's Public Utilities Commission and the federal government actually require of movers. Read the one you need, check the mover yourself, and only then collect quotes.

The short version. Every household goods mover working inside Colorado must hold a PUC permit whose number begins with HHG, and must give you a written estimate and a written contract. For a move that crosses a state line, the federal rules apply instead. If a company cannot show you the matching registration, stop talking to it.

Quick answers

How do I check whether a Denver mover is legal?

For a move that stays inside Colorado, search the mover on the Colorado Public Utilities Commission permit search; a household goods mover's permit number starts with HHG and must appear on its ads and contracts. For a move across state lines, look up the company's USDOT number in the federal Protect Your Move tools.

What paperwork does a Colorado mover have to give me?

The PUC says every permitted household goods mover must provide a written estimate and a written contract. The estimate must list total costs and the basis for them, and the PUC notes it is due at least 24 hours before the move unless you booked with less than 24 hours notice.
